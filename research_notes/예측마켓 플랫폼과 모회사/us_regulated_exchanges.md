## Q1. Which entities currently operate CFTC-registered DCMs and/or DCOs that list event contracts? (verified map)

### Takeaway
As of late Sept 2026, at least 12 distinct CFTC-registered venues are live with event contracts. Kalshi, Polymarket US (ex-QCEX), ForecastEx (IBKR), Nadex/Crypto.com Derivatives North America (now under the OG.com spin-off), Rothera (Robinhood–Susquehanna, ex-MIAXdx/LedgerX), Gemini Titan/Olympus, Railbird (DraftKings), Aristotle Exchange (now Underdog), Ludlow Exchange (Novig), ProphetX, CME (FanDuel Predicts) and Bitnomial are live. Kraken (Small Exchange) and Coinbase (own DCM plus The Clearing Company) hold licences, but their own event-contract listings were not confirmed. Sporttrade and Smarkets are applicants. PredictIt is still a no-action academic market, and Cboe's "Cboe Predicts" is SEC-regulated, so it is not a CFTC venue.

### Inferences
- The field has split three ways. (a) Independent, venture-backed "pure-play" DCM+DCOs: Kalshi, Polymarket US, ProphetX, Novig. (b) Broker/fintech-owned exchanges built mainly to internalise their own retail flow: ForecastEx/IBKR, Rothera/Robinhood, Gemini, Kraken, Coinbase, DraftKings/Railbird, Underdog/Aristotle. (c) Incumbent exchanges entering carefully: CME via FanDuel, and Cboe via SEC-regulated options. For a data vendor this means each exchange group has its own buyer: exchange product/market-ops teams at (a), and parent-level product/data teams at (b) and (c).
- Almost every DCM licence was bought rather than applied for from scratch (QCEX, MIAXdx, Nadex, Small Exchange, Railbird, Aristotle). Weather or settlement-data relationships built with the *old* licensee may not carry over to the new owner, so contacts should target the current parent.
- The failed Crypto.com–Small Exchange leg and IG's later sale to Kraken are inferred from two separate sources and are not confirmed by any single source.

## Q2. Ownership, major investors, latest valuation, key public executives, headquarters (entity by entity)

### Takeaway
The largest venues have become multi-billion-dollar companies with strategic shareholders. Kalshi was valued at $22B in its 2026 Series F and was reported in talks at ~$40B. Polymarket was valued at $21B post-money (2026-08-31), with ICE reported as its largest shareholder (~22%). OG.com has a $5B standalone valuation (2026-09), and Robinhood took equity in it. Rothera is a Robinhood-controlled JV with Susquehanna. ForecastEx is wholly inside IBKR, and Gemini is inside public Gemini Space Station, Inc. Named decision-makers relevant to a data vendor include: Kalshi's co-founders plus a Head of Partnerships; Polymarket's CEO plus its new US-exchange compliance and risk chiefs; ForecastEx's CEO and CRO; Crypto.com's Global Head of Predictions; and Rothera's CEO. Headquarters are mostly missing from the search results (see Gaps).

## Q3. Regulatory status as of Sept 2026: registrations, self-certified contract types, state litigation, 2026 CFTC actions

### Takeaway
2026 brought four key federal actions: a March 2026 ANPRM plus a supportive staff advisory (26-08); a June 2026 NPRM rewriting Rule 40.11 public-interest review around "enumerated activities" (gaming, war, terrorism, assassination, unlawful activity); a July 2026 manipulation settlement (Santos/Kalshi); and a September 2026 staff advisory (26-27) presuming "mention" contracts susceptible to manipulation. In the courts there is a widening circuit split on sports contracts. The 3rd Circuit favoured Kalshi on preemption (April 2026). The 9th Circuit sided with tribes (IGRA, 2026-09-16), and the 6th Circuit sided with Ohio and Tennessee (2026-09-25/26), which may push the question to the Supreme Court. Weather contracts are not the target of any of these actions.

### Cited Findings
**CFTC actions, 2026**
- **ANPRM (2026-03-12; Federal Register 2026-03-16, doc 2026-05105):** Asks about applying core principles and regulations to prediction markets, "the types of event contracts that may be prohibited as contrary to the public interest," cost-benefit and other topics. Comments were due 2026-04-30. The Advisory Letter plus the ANPRM signal that the CFTC "intends to exercise exclusive jurisdiction ... despite continued legal challenges from states, such as Nevada and Arizona" — [CFTC PR 9194-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9194-26); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/16/2026-05105/prediction-markets); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260316-cftc-issues-notable-prediction-markets-advisory); [SBA Office of Advocacy 2026-03-23](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2026/03/23/cftc-prediction-markets/) [search summary — primary not opened]
- **Staff Advisory Letter No. 26-08 (2026-03-12, Division of Market Oversight):** Signals a supportive stance toward prediction markets and event-based derivatives, "including contracts based on sporting events," if products are carefully designed with strong compliance, surveillance and integrity controls — [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/03/cftc-issues-new-guidance-for-prediction-markets/) [search summary — primary not opened]
- **NPRM "Prediction Markets; Public Interest Determinations" (issued 2026-06-10; Federal Register 2026-06-12, doc 2026-11854):**
  - It amends Rule 40.11 with a three-step test: (1) is it an event contract in an excluded commodity; (2) does it "involve" an enumerated activity (unlawful activity, terrorism, assassination, war, gaming, or similar); (3) is it contrary to the public interest.
  - It restructures the 90-day review with milestones, consolidation authority, written findings, and "deemed-concluded" consequences if no adverse order issues.
  - Comments were due 2026-07-27, and the rule takes effect 60 days after final publication.
  - Law-firm summaries cite total trading volume across CFTC DCMs of more than $25B in 2025.

### Inferences
- **Weather contracts sit outside the current regulatory fire.** The NPRM's enumerated activities (gaming, war, terrorism, assassination, unlawful activity) and the litigation (sports as gaming) do not reach temperature, rainfall, snowfall or hurricane contracts. Weather is therefore among the lowest-regulatory-risk categories an exchange can grow into.
- The 26-27 advisory makes "independently generated" and "externally verifiable" settlement data the benchmark for staff scrutiny. Instrument-based weather observations fit this well, but the Paris CDG tampering case (Q4) shows that a *single* physical station can be interfered with. A vendor offering multi-station cross-verification and tamper-evident data is well aligned with the CFTC's stated concern.
- If the Supreme Court takes up the sports question, exchanges may diversify away from sports toward "non-gaming" categories (economics, climate/weather), which could raise demand for weather settlement sources. This is a directional inference, not a forecast.

## Q4. Weather and climate contracts: who lists them, and what settlement/resolution source each uses

### Takeaway
Kalshi has the deepest weather complex and settles on named government sources: NWS Daily Climate Report (daily high/low), NWS climatological and NOWData products (monthly rain and snow), and the NHC (hurricanes). It also uses one commercial vendor, The Weather Company, for hourly-temperature markets. ForecastEx (IBKR) lists daily-high contracts for about 20 US cities, settled from airport ASOS/METAR hourly observations, and is adding international cities (Madrid, Paris, Vancouver, Montreal). It also lists NOAA monthly US temperature and NOAA global-temperature-anomaly contracts. Polymarket (international) runs daily-high markets in about 44–55 cities, resolved from NOAA weather.gov station time series with a Weather Underground fallback (Hong Kong via the Hong Kong Observatory). A weather-data site says Robinhood and IBKR resolve from Weather Underground's "History" tab. CME weather futures (not event contracts) use Speedwell Settlement Services processing NWS data. Crypto.com lists only a thin set (e.g., "hottest year on record"). No weather listings were found at Gemini, Rothera, Railbird, Novig, ProphetX, Bitnomial or Underdog.
