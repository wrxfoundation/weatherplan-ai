## 1. South Korea — legal status of prediction markets, regulator blocks, Korean exchanges

### Takeaway
Korea's online-content regulator ordered Polymarket blocked on **2026-08-18**. The regulator is now 방송미디어통신심의위원회 ("방미심위"); English press still calls it the KCSC. It found Polymarket to be a de facto illegal gambling venue under the **Criminal Act (형법)** and the **National Sports Promotion Act (국민체육진흥법)**. It explicitly listed **weather** among the "uncontrollable events" behind that finding.

Kalshi had not been referred or reviewed as of that date. No Korean exchange (Upbit/Dunamu, Bithumb, Coinone, Korbit) was found running a real prediction market. No FSC/FSS position on event contracts under the Capital Markets Act was found.

### Inferences
- **Weather is not a regulatory safe harbour in Korea.** The regulator named weather among the uncontrollable events behind its gambling finding. A Seoul-weather contract offered to Korean residents would likely be analysed the same way. [LEGAL REVIEW]
- Selling index or settlement data to a platform the regulator has labelled a gambling venue could raise **aiding (도박 방조)** or reputational questions for a Korean company. The risk is highest if the data settles Seoul contracts that Koreans can reach. [LEGAL REVIEW]
- Kalshi is currently the least sensitive of the two large platforms from a Korean standpoint. It is CFTC-regulated, has not been referred to the regulator, and is reportedly not geo-restricted for Korea. However, the regulator's reasoning is platform-agnostic, so a future referral is plausible.
- The Korean exchanges look unlikely to buy weather-settlement data for prediction products in the near term, given the 2026-08-18 ruling and the absence of any Korean exchange product.

### Gaps
- No FSC/FSS statement was found on whether event contracts count as financial investment products or derivatives under the Capital Markets Act (자본시장법).
- Nothing was found on the Act on Special Cases concerning Regulation and Punishment of Speculative Acts (사행행위 등 규제 및 처벌 특례법) or on the stance of the 사행산업통합감독위원회 (NGCC).
- It is unknown whether Korean exchanges restrict withdrawals to Polymarket-linked addresses, for example through the Travel Rule whitelist.
- The official English name of the reorganised 방송미디어통신심의위원회 was not confirmed.
- It is unknown whether other platforms (Kalshi, Opinion, Predict.fun, Limitless, etc.) were referred after 2026-08-18.
- The details of Bithumb's "가격 예측 서비스" are unknown.

## 2. Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong, Taiwan, India, Southeast Asia — status, notable platforms, blocks

### Takeaway
Asia is uniformly restrictive toward crypto prediction markets:
- **Singapore**: GRA block, from late 2024 or early 2025.
- **Thailand**: blocked from January 2025.
- **Taiwan**: blocked since December 2023, with prosecutions continuing in 2026.
- **India**: PROGA in force from 2026-05-01, with Section 69A blocking of Polymarket and Kalshi.
- **Korea**: blocked from 2026-08.
- **Hong Kong**: an SFC-linked "may be illegal gambling" warning in 2026-04, but no block was found.
- **Japan**: the FSA told the Diet on 2026-04-21 that no law covers prediction markets and they may be gambling. Polymarket is still targeting Japanese authorization by 2030.
- **Vietnam, the Philippines, Indonesia and Malaysia**: no official action found.

## 3. Asian crypto exchanges and groups with prediction products or investments

### Takeaway
By late September 2026 most large Asia-facing exchanges offered a prediction product, in one of three models:
1. **BNB Chain venues in the Binance / YZi Labs orbit**:
   - Binance Wallet with Predict.fun (2026-04) and its API (2026-06).
   - The YZi-backed, Hong Kong-based Opinion (2025-10).
   - The PancakeSwap/YZi-backed Probable (2025-12).
2. **Front-ends on Polymarket liquidity**: Bybit Prediction, Byreal Predict, Bitget Wallet and KuCoin Web3 Wallet.
3. **In-house event contracts**: OKX Event Contracts (2026-04, Asia/LatAm/CIS) and Gate Prediction Markets, plus Gate's white-label Event Contracts Builder.

Crypto.com's OG is a **US-only**, CFTC-regulated product. No Korean or Japanese exchange product was found.

### Cited Findings

## 4. Europe / UK — Betfair, Smarkets, Matchbook; Kalshi/Polymarket availability and bans; MiCA; UKGC stance

### Takeaway
Continental regulators mostly treat Polymarket and Kalshi as unlicensed gambling:
- Polymarket is blocked or restricted in France, Belgium, Poland, Italy, the Netherlands, Portugal, Germany and others.
- Spain reportedly ordered ISPs to block Kalshi in 2026-05.

The UK instead offers a **licensing route**. The Gambling Commission classified most prediction markets as "Betting Intermediaries" on 2026-02-04. The FCA was reported on 2026-09-07 to be weighing an easing for financial-event contracts. Flutter's Betfair ("Betfair Predicts", UK beta from 2026-04-08) and Matchbook are building UK-licensed prediction products. In late September 2026 Polymarket was lobbying European regulators to be treated as a financial service.

### Inferences
- Asian-city weather contracts today live mainly on **Polymarket**. They settle on **airport-station data republished by Weather Underground** (Seoul/Incheon, Tokyo/Haneda, Singapore/Changi) or on a **national met service** (Hong Kong Observatory).

- However, the regulator's 2026-08-18 ruling explicitly cited weather, so supplying Polymarket's Seoul settlement data is the **most legally sensitive** deal type for a Korean company. [LEGAL REVIEW]
- Polymarket's use of the Hong Kong Observatory shows it will adopt a national-met-service source where one is readily available. Regulated venues (Kalshi) anchor on government climate reports (NWS) in the US. A private Korean data company would likely need to argue **independence, auditability and station comparability** to become a primary settlement source. It might more realistically act as a secondary or verification source, or as a data vendor to market makers.
- If Kalshi really lists Seoul, Tokyo or Singapore, the most useful question for Kalshi is **which source settles those series**.
