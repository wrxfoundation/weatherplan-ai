## 1. Exchange ambassador and affiliate programs: structure, tiers, rewards, activities and status

### Takeaway
Exchanges use the word "ambassador" for two different models:
- **Recognition programs.** These are unpaid or invite-only: Binance Angels, Coinbase's CDP Ambassador Program and Bybit's 2025 "#IMakeIt" merch-ambassador campaign. They reward people with swag, event roles and access to the team.
- **Commission programs.** These are affiliate tiers, often relabelled as "ambassador" tiers. They pay 20–50% of referred users' trading fees, and up to 70% on MEXC's live-stream and KOL tracks. Entry depends on follower counts or trading volume, tiers are reassessed regularly, and some countries are excluded.

The newest launches I found (KuCoin 2024, MEXC November 2025) use the commission model.

### Inferences
- **Most exchange "ambassador" programs are affiliate marketing.** They add a status layer on top. Tiers, volume thresholds, periodic reassessment and "level protection" are all built around sharing trading-fee revenue. Only a few programs pay nothing per referral: Binance Angels, the CDP Ambassador Program, and probably Bybit's #IMakeIt.
- **Unpaid programs run on access and status.** They offer swag, VIP or organiser roles at events, contact with senior staff and early product access. They recruit people who are already fans or proven builders, so they depend on a strong existing brand. They also act as a talent pipeline: Binance pairs volunteer Angels with paid Accelerator roles.
- **Programs that recruit students or builders are about identity, not sales.** Bybit #IMakeIt and Coinbase CDP fall into this group. They are the closest exchange equivalents to a "friendlier community face".
- **Korea is often fenced out.** Offshore exchanges exclude certain countries from even their volunteer programs; Binance Angels excludes Korea. These programs are not templates built for a Korea-first project.
- **Diversity appears only in wording.** The only diversity-related language I found in exchange ambassador copy is Binance's "all walks of life… ethnicities". I found no gender targets in any exchange ambassador program. Bitget's women-focused program is covered separately in §5.

## 2. Why commission-based models are unsuitable or risky for a non-exchange project

### Takeaway
Exchange commissions come out of trading-fee revenue, which a DePIN device project does not have. Copying the model would mean paying per sign-up, per device sale or in tokens. That creates four risks:
- Ambassadors become financial promoters, subject to promotion rules. The UK has banned refer-a-friend incentives for cryptoassets since 8 October 2023.
- It brings in the registration and geo-restriction problems that already keep offshore exchange programs out of Korea.
- Sub-affiliate commissions create multi-level structures.
- It clashes with wellbian's own rule against promising income.

### Cited Findings
- **UK ban on referral incentives.** From 8 October 2023, the FCA's cryptoasset promotion rules ban firms from offering "any monetary or non-monetary benefits which incentivise investment activity". This includes "refer a friend" rewards and new-joiner bonuses. The rules also add a cooling-off period for first-time investors, require checks that consumers have appropriate knowledge and experience, and require clear risk warnings and promotions that are "clear, fair and not misleading". — [P] [FCA press release](https://fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-introduces-tough-new-rules-marketing-cryptoassets); [S] [Caproasia](https://www.caproasia.com/2023/06/10/uk-financial-conduct-authority-introduces-new-advertising-rules-for-crypto-assets-cooling-off-period-for-1st-time-investors-from-8th-october-2023-ban-on-refer-a-friend-bonus/)
- **The FCA's reasoning.** "incentives to invest can unduly influence consumers' investment decisions and cause them to invest without fully considering the risks involved." — [S] law-firm summaries, mapping uncertain: [Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2023/financial-conduct-authority-introduces-new-financial-promotions-rules-for-cryptocurrencies/), [Stephenson Harwood](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/insights/the-uk-financial-conduct-authoritys-new-rules-in-relation-to-the-regulation-of-financial-promotions-in-respect-of-cryptoassets/)
- **What the FCA ban does and does not cover.**
  - It also covers "shareholder benefits", such as discounts tied to the cryptoassets someone holds.
  - Benefits that are "intrinsic to a cryptoasset or exclusively bound up with its function", such as governance voting rights, are not treated as an incentive.
  - [S] [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102iho2/new-regulations-for-cryptoasset-promotions-finally-land); [S] [Stephenson Harwood](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/insights/the-uk-financial-conduct-authoritys-new-rules-in-relation-to-the-regulation-of-financial-promotions-in-respect-of-cryptoassets/)
- **Korea's FIU and offshore exchanges (2022).** Korea's Financial Intelligence Unit (금융정보분석원) notified 16 offshore exchanges that serving Korean residents requires reporting under the Specified Financial Transaction Information Act (특금법). The exchanges included MEXC, KuCoin, Phemex, XT.com, Bitrue, ZB.com, Bitglobal, CoinW and CoinEx. One summary says MEXC and KuCoin kept offering Korean-language service afterwards. — [S] [Metro Seoul, 2022-08-15](https://www.metroseoul.co.kr/article/20220815500167); [S] [Tokenpost](https://www.tokenpost.kr/news/breaking/237790) (mapping uncertain)
- **App-store blocking in Korea.** Google Play blocks apps of overseas exchanges that have not registered in Korea from 28 January. The article names Binance and OKX. The year is not shown in the summary. — [S] [Cryptonews Korea](https://cryptonews.com/kr/news/google-play-to-block-binance-okx-from-korea-starting-jan-28/)
- **Exchanges fence their own programs by country.**
  - Binance Angels excludes Korea and several other countries "due to local regulations". — [P] [Lever](https://jobs.lever.co/binance/cd108578-1fc7-4a29-a8a8-49dcd179007c)
  - Bybit affiliates may not promote in the US, Mainland China or Singapore. — [S] [UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-program-directory/bybit/)
  - MEXC Live is limited to CIS countries and Ukraine. — [P] [MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/campaigns/recurt-live)
- **Two-level payouts.** Bybit affiliates earn an extra 10% of their sub-affiliates' earnings. — [S] [UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-program-directory/bybit/)
- **wellbian's internal rules (internal source).**
  - No income promises (no "월 ○원") and no price outlooks.
  - Rewards are described as "테스트 중이며 지급량·가치 비보장" (in testing; amount and value not guaranteed).
  - WLBN is not to be called a coin.
  - Source: `depin/CLAUDE.md`, 핵심 금지 section.

### Inferences
- **There is no fee pool to share.** Any wellbian "commission" would come from device sales or token issuance. Paying ambassadors a cut for selling devices to friends and family is direct selling. Adding Bybit-style sub-affiliate cuts would create a multi-level structure, which needs Korean legal review (see Gaps).
- **Commission pulls ambassadors toward return-on-investment talk.** That conflicts with wellbian's no-income-promise rule and with FCA-style "fair, clear, not misleading" standards. When volunteers overstate rewards, the project's brand carries the risk.
- **Token-based commissions make every ambassador a token promoter.** Korean regulators already police offshore exchanges marketing to residents (FIU notices, app blocks). A Korea-first project should keep ambassador pay in fixed KRW stipends and non-cash recognition, separate from sign-ups and sales.
- **Volume-ranked commissions reward people who already have large audiences.** Crypto ownership skews male (39% women vs 61% men, per Triple-A 2024, cited by CFOtech in §5). So commission tiers would likely amplify existing male-heavy KOL audiences rather than create a "more diverse, friendlier face".
- **Better-fitting models found in this research:**
  - The Korean supporter model: a flat stipend, a 수료증 (certificate), awards and an internship link (§3–§4).
  - Binance Angels and CDP-style access perks (§1).
  - Team1 and Superteam-style bounties for specific deliverables such as content, translation or events, paid whether or not anything is sold (§3).
- **Tokens for operating a sensor are a different case.** Operating rewards for running a sensor may count as "intrinsic to function" under FCA logic. Referral bonuses would not. This is a legal question, not a research finding.

### Gaps
- **Korean law was not researched** because the search budget ran out. It needs counsel or the rules/risk workstream. Relevant statutes: 방문판매법 (rules on 다단계 multi-level marketing and 후원방문판매 sponsored door-to-door sales); 유사수신행위법 (unauthorised fund-raising); 표시광고법 and the KFTC 추천·보증 심사지침 (paid-endorsement disclosure); and 가상자산이용자보호법 (virtual asset user protection).
- I found no documented case of a DePIN or other non-exchange project being sanctioned over a commission-based ambassador program.
- Whether selling wellbian devices counts as a "financial promotion" in any jurisdiction is an open legal question.

## 3. Korean crypto ambassador, 서포터즈, 홍보대사 and 기자단 programs (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
**Korean exchange programs** are cohorts of university students doing PR. Examples: Upbit's 업투 (UpTo), Bithumb's 썸즈업 (Thumbs Up), GOPAX's 가디언즈 (Guardians) and the Busan Digital Asset Exchange's Beyond Busan. They pay fixed monthly 활동비 (activity allowances) of ₩200,000–300,000 and run 4–5 months. Participants work in teams and receive a 수료증, prize money and an internship opportunity. None of them pays referral commissions.

**Korean chains and Korean chapters of global chains** take a different approach. Kaia, Superteam Korea (Solana), Avalanche Team1 Korea and Solana University offer expenses-paid representation at events, bounties, event funding, hackathons and possible jobs.

### Inferences
- **Two Korean archetypes:**
  - **(A) The exchange or corporate "서포터즈".** A cohort of university students, paid in KRW, focused on PR, content, offline campaigns and CSR. It comes with awards and an internship link, builds the brand and carries little reputational risk.
  - **(B) The global-chain chapter.** Builder-focused, using bounties, grants and hackathons, with expenses-paid representation and possible employment.
- **None of the Korean programs found uses referral commissions.**
- **Korean crypto supporter programs use an education and protection framing.** GOPAX: investor protection. Upbit: "healthy investment culture". Beyond Busan: helping people with limited digital access. That framing appears to be what makes a supporter program socially acceptable in Korea.
- **Cohort sizes vary widely:** about 20 (Bithumb), about 30 (Upbit), about 700 (Beyond Busan, publicly backed).
- **Market-standard settings for a small project:** a cohort of 10–20 people, 4 months, ₩200,000–300,000 a month, a 수료증, awards, and an internship or hiring path. Whether wellbian or KWeather can offer an internship is a management decision.
- **Age limits narrow the pool.** Bithumb 3기 was limited to "20대" (people in their 20s) enrolled at a university. A student-only design limits the community face to one age group. For an indoor-air product, a non-student track (parents, teachers, facility managers) may reach a broader and more mixed group.

### Gaps
- None of these programs reports the gender make-up of its cohorts, so there is no data on diversity outcomes.
- No programs found for WEMIX, Korbit or Coinone, or for Korean chapters of Sui, Aptos, Polygon or Story. Not checked: Kaia's current recruitment status and dates.
- Selection processes (documents, interviews) and required activity quotas (for example, posts per month) were not captured.
- 2026 cohorts (업투 5기; any 썸즈업 4기 or later) are unknown. The Blockmedia tag page suggests a September 2026 item.

## 4. Korean corporate and university supporter-program norms (non-crypto baseline)

### Takeaway
Large companies' student supporter programs follow a common pattern:
- **Stipend:** about ₩200,000 a month (some ₩300,000), the same for everyone.
- **Term:** 4–6 months.
- **Activities:** content production plus offline missions, with training, a launch ceremony (발대식) and a completion ceremony (수료식).
- **Recognition:** a 수료증 for those who meet the activity conditions, plus monthly and final awards for teams and individuals.
- **Career benefits:** mentoring, an experiential internship (체험형 인턴), or exemption from document screening (서류전형 면제) in hiring.

### Cited Findings
- **LG U+ "유대감" (대학생 감성 서포터즈):**
  - ₩200,000 a month per person.
  - Training in content planning and production during the program.
  - Monthly awards for the best individuals and teams.
  - An official 수료증 for everyone at the end, plus a separate award for the best team.
  - Sources: [P] LG newsroom releases for the 5th to 9th cohorts (mapping to a specific cohort or date uncertain): [5기](https://www.lg.co.kr/media/release/20901), [6기](https://www.lg.co.kr/media/release/21645), [7기](https://lg.co.kr/media/release/22423), [8기](https://www.lg.co.kr/media/release/23277), [9기](https://www.lg.co.kr/media/release/23858)
- **우체국 (Korea Post) 서포터즈.** Six months (April–September). Monthly 활동비, team apparel, souvenirs and a 수료증. Top performers receive a certificate of commendation and prizes. — [S] [Kyongbuk Maeil, 2025-05-21](https://kbmaeil.com/article/20250521500460) (mapping uncertain)
- **대학혁신지원사업 총괄협의회 서포터즈 (2020, older).** A monthly activity allowance (paid as a scholarship) plus incentives; a 수료증 for meeting the activity conditions; and awards for top supporters at a national university-innovation forum. — [P] [Sahmyook University notice](https://www.syu.ac.kr/?p=83364)
- **한국투자신탁운용 (Korea Investment Management) ACE ETF 대학생 서포터즈:**
  - 1기 (August 2025): the best performer is exempt from document screening in new-graduate recruitment. About 5 months of SNS and video content. Duties are admissions PR, modelling, protocol and events. — [P] [Sahmyook University](https://www.syu.ac.kr/blog/%ED%95%99%EC%83%9D%ED%99%8D%EB%B3%B4%EB%8C%80%EC%82%AC%EB%8B%A8-%EC%88%98%EC%95%B0%EB%B0%B0%EC%84%9C%EB%8D%94-13%EA%B8%B0-%EC%8B%A0%EC%9E%85-%EB%AA%A8%EC%A7%91-%EA%B3%B5%EA%B3%A0)

  - Upbit: ₩200,000 a month, about 4 months, ₩12M prize pool, internship experience.
  - Bithumb: ₩300,000 a month, 4 months, ₩5M team prize, four individual prizes of ₩2M, and an internship or ambassador path.
  - GOPAX: 수료증, mentoring and a team-building budget; no cash stated.

### Inferences
- **Summary of norms** (from the programs above, not from a platform-wide survey):

  | Item | Typical value |
  |---|---|
  | 활동비 | ₩200,000–300,000 a month, the same amount for every participant |
  | Term | 4–6 months, with a 발대식 and a 수료식 |
  | Format | Teams of 3–4; cohorts of 20–30 in companies (Busan's publicly backed program is about 700) |
  | Deliverables | Monthly content (SNS, video) plus offline campaigns, events and CSR, with training |
  | Recognition | 수료증 if activity conditions are met; monthly and final awards. Crypto exchanges' prizes: about ₩5M per team, ₩2M per individual, total pools of ₩5M–12M |
  | Career link | Mentoring and lectures → 체험형 인턴 (Upbit, Bithumb) → 서류전형 면제 (Korea Investment Management) |

- **Illustrative budget (my arithmetic, not a sourced figure).** 20 people × ₩250,000 × 4 months = ₩20M. Add an awards pool of ₩5M–12M, plus welcome kits and events.
- **A flat stipend for everyone is the norm in Korea.** This works in favour of diversity: it avoids the audience-size bias of commission tiers, and it gives equal pay across genders automatically.
- **Students value certificates and career links most.** The strongest draws are an experiential internship and document-screening exemption. A KWeather-linked internship, if HR agrees, would be the most distinctive offer a small project could make.

### Gaps
- No platform-wide figure for average 활동비, because 링커리어 (Linkareer), 캠퍼스픽 (Campuspick) and 씽굿 (Thinkyou) pages were not opened. The figures above come from individual programs.
- Not captured: activity quotas (for example, posts per month), selection steps (documents, interviews), dropout and penalty rules, and the dates of each LG U+ cohort.
- Supporter norms for groups other than students were not researched: parents, homemakers (주부), citizens, teachers. These may matter for an indoor-air product.

## 5. Gender diversity in crypto: organisations, women-focused cohorts and campaigns, women creators as community hosts, and what is documented as working

### Takeaway
The documented approaches fall into five groups:
1. Education cohorts funded by scholarships (SheFi; Binance Charity's 36,000+ scholarships for women).
2. Community spaces for women and non-binary people (Boys Club; Women in Web3 Korea).
3. Visibility, role-model and funding programs run by exchanges (Bitget Blockchain4Her: a $10M pledge, an ambassador cohort and awards).
4. Programming led by women inside major conferences (KBW Women in Tech, hosted by Hashed).
5. Advocacy for balanced speaker line-ups (AWiC's #UnmanelYourPanel).

The evidence of effectiveness is almost entirely self-reported reach. I found no outcome evaluations. I also found no program that specifically recruits women creators as community hosts.

### Cited Findings

**Baseline data**

- **AWiC State of Inclusion Report 2024.** The Association for Women in Cryptocurrency (AWiC) produced it with the DEI platform Diversio.
  - Survey of 500+ respondents from 200+ firms.
  - Crypto, blockchain and Web3 scored 43.9 on inclusion, against 78 for the tech industry overall.
  - 82% of women reported experiences of harassment, compared with 48% of men.
  - It was announced alongside AWiC's #UnmanelYourPanel advocacy initiative.
  - Sources: [P] [press release, Newswire.ca](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/association-for-women-in-cryptocurrency-celebrates-international-women-s-day-with-inclusion-survey-results-and-unmanelyourpanel-advocacy-initiative-809597205.html); [P] [AAP, 2024-03-07](https://aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20240307ae56047/); [S] [Global Fintech Series](https://globalfintechseries.com/cryptocurrency/association-for-women-in-cryptocurrency-inaugural-report-finds-noteworthy-inclusion-gaps-in-crypto-industry/)
  - **Date conflict:** one summary says the report was "released on March 26th", while the press release is dated 7 March 2024.
- **Other statistics, as cited by CFOtech for International Women's Day 2025 (secondary citations of other studies):**
  - Women were 39% of crypto owners in 2024 vs 61% men, up from 37% (Triple-A).
  - Only 8.6% of crypto startups are led by women (Bitget study).
  - 27% of employees at top crypto startups were women (BCG, 2022).
  - 27% of women in the field had started a crypto startup, vs 41% of men (KuCoin Web3 Career Market report).
  - 81% of women cite lack of education as a barrier.
  - Source: [S] [CFOtech](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/iwd-2025-web3-aims-at-mass-adoption-but-it-won-t-happen-without-women). Also see [KuCoin survey release, 2022-07-26](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220726005099/en/KuCoin-Survey-Reveals-That-Females-Are-Showing-Great-Potential-in-Driving-the-Development-of-Web-3) [P, older].
- **Korea Times (2024-10-19):**
  - Women hold 26% of jobs in the crypto sector and 6% of leadership roles. The underlying source is not stated.
  - Language in online forums "can feel harsh and exclusive".
  - Meetups and community events are often held in nightclubs, which is another barrier.
  - Women mentors and role models are hard to find in Web3.
  - Source: [S] [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/cryptocurrency/20241019/pioneering-korean-women-embrace-web3-opportunities)
- **Workforce figures don't agree:** 27% (BCG, top startups), 26% of jobs (Korea Times), and a further 12.7% of "the Web3 workforce" that appeared in one summary (mapping uncertain: [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/crypto-sexism-women-in-crypto) / [Blockhead, 2022](https://blockhead.co/2022/10/01/crypto-bros-suck-but-is-web3-bad-for-diversity/)). They use different definitions, so they can't be compared.
- **Bitget's two numbers measure different things.** One study says women-led blockchain startups got only 6% of total funding ([P] [AAP, 2024-01-16](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20240116ae11779/)). Another says 8.6% of crypto startups are women-led (via CFOtech, above). One is a share of funding, the other a share of startups.
- Hacken published a "2024 Gender Equality Report" (content not retrieved). — [S] [Hacken](https://hacken.io/insights/2024-gender-equality-report/)

### Inferences
- **What has some documented support, mostly reach rather than outcomes:**
  - **Education with funded seats.** SheFi and Binance Charity directly address the most-cited barrier, "lack of education" (81%).
  - **Separate safe communities.** Boys Club, WiW3K and women-only groups on LinkedIn and Facebook (§6) let women take part without being harassed.
  - **Women-led programming inside flagship events.** KBW Women in Tech (Hashed) is the Korean example.
  - **Role models and funding.** Bitget's B4H.
  - **Commitments to balanced speaker line-ups.** AWiC's #UnmanelYourPanel.
- **Partnering is quicker and more credible than building alone for an all-male team.** Options include:
  - Co-hosting with WiW3K or a KBW-style Women in Tech track.
  - Sponsoring scholarship seats in an education cohort, for example an "indoor air + Web3 basics" cohort.
  - Using existing women-focused XRPL learning content (Ripple × AllBright WAVE) as reference material. **Caution:** under `depin/CLAUDE.md`, Ripple may be mentioned only as RLUSD issuer and XRPL contributor. Nothing should imply a wellbian × Ripple collaboration.
- **Women's visibility should come from substantive roles, not casting.** Examples: leading education sessions, helping with sensor installation, leading content, giving product feedback, hosting AMAs or meet-ups. Combine this with equal stipends and equal credit. The Korean flat-stipend norm (§4) already supports pay parity.
- **Indoor air quality reaches homes, schools and childcare settings.** The internal sales strategy already mentions a "시설→학부모" funnel (from facilities to parents) in `depin/CLAUDE.md`. Parent, caregiver and teacher tracks may broaden participation naturally. Use gender-neutral wording ("parents, caregivers, teachers") to avoid stereotyping (see the perfume pitfall in §6).
- **Measure outcomes, not reach.** Track cohort composition (optional self-report), retention, the share of AMAs and events hosted by women, and harassment reports and how they were resolved.

### Gaps
- Not researched because the search budget ran out: She256, Women in Blockchain, CryptoChicks, Black Women Blockchain Council and similar global organisations; women-focused programs by layer-1 chains (Solana, Polygon, Avalanche and others); and KBW 2024–2026 women's events.
- No evaluations found that measure the outcomes of women-focused crypto programs (retention, career moves, community composition).
- Not found: Korean women-in-Web3 organisations other than WiW3K, and WiW3K's current activity.
- Not captured: WAVE's launch date and whether it is still running; B4H's 2025–26 status.
- No documented programs of women creators hosting crypto communities, and no data on how audiences respond to them.

## 6. Pitfalls: tokenism, sexualised promotion ("booth babes", strip-club venues), harassment and the moderation it requires

### Takeaway
Three failure modes recur:
1. **Sexualised venues and promotional models.** Examples: the North American Bitcoin Conference networking party in 2018, and the 2026 Consensus after-party. In 2026 a sponsor said it would reconsider its sponsorship, and a brand whose logo appeared said it had no role in the party.
2. **Stereotyped or token gestures.** Gendered products (Binance's 2024 perfume), women's panels pushed to side tents, and one-day International Women's Day campaigns.
3. **Harassment of visible women.** 82% of women in the industry report experiences of harassment, and women creators have been mass-reported.

Documented mitigations:
- Dress-code and venue standards. The RSA Conference's 2015 rule was later described as "no big deal".
- Organisers acknowledging mistakes and committing to do better.
- Commitments to balanced speaker line-ups.
- Confidential reporting channels and trained moderators.
- Women-only spaces as a refuge.

### Cited Findings

**Sexualised venues and promotional models**

- **North American Bitcoin Conference, Miami, January 2018.**
  - The organiser held a networking event at a strip club (E11EVEN). It was open to all attendees and sponsored by Dash.
  - When the event ended at 11 p.m., nude performances resumed and many attendees stayed, still wearing their conference badges.
  - Several women who attended said the party made them uncomfortable, and many chose not to go. One attendee told Bloomberg: "There was a message being sent to women, that, 'OK, this isn't really your place.'"
  - The organiser first defended the choice, then told Bloomberg it regretted it: "Having the networking party at E11even was a misstep… We always aim to be as inclusive as possible and create a safe environment." The conference later pledged to "do better".

**Harassment and moderation**

- **Harassment rates.** 82% of women in crypto reported experiences of harassment, compared with 48% of men. ### Inferences
- **The two highest-impact mistakes an all-male team could make** are using promotional models or appearance-based "faces", and choosing nightclub or after-party formats. Both lead to documented sponsor backlash (2018, 2026). They are also a specific barrier in Korea (Korea Times). Daytime, family-friendly formats suit an indoor-air product better.
- **Control how the brand is used at side events.** Consensys's 2026 statement shows that a logo can appear at a damaging side event without the brand's involvement. Partner and side-event agreements should cover how the logo is used.
- **Signs of tokenism to avoid:**
  - A one-off International Women's Day campaign.
  - A separate "women's panel" while the main stage stays all-male.
  - Stereotyped products or framing.
  - An all-executive "women ambassador" showcase with no grassroots roles.

  Better: balanced main-stage and AMA line-ups, continuing roles, and decision input (for example, ambassadors give product and community feedback that is acted on).
- **Put safety in place before raising visibility.** Featuring women hosts publicly without protection exposes them to the harassment and mass-reporting documented above. Minimum set-up:
  - A written code of conduct covering Telegram, X, Discord and offline events.
  - A confidential reporting channel and a named response owner.
  - Moderator training and fast enforcement.
  - Allowing pseudonymous participation, and not requiring ambassadors to publish personal handles (also consistent with wellbian's no-PII rule in `depin/CLAUDE.md`).
  - Clear escalation for offline incidents.
- **A Korean-style supporter cohort fits.** It has flat pay, team projects, a 수료증 and awards. Combined with selection that deliberately aims for gender balance (applied internally rather than marketed as a "women's program"), it avoids both the male skew of commission tiers and the "tokenism" label.

### Gaps
- No documented controversies found over booth or racing models (부스 모델, 레이싱 모델) at Korean blockchain events. Korean searches returned only general event coverage.
- No crypto-specific data on how well moderation works (for example, how codes of conduct on Telegram or Discord change women's participation).
- Not researched: Korean legal obligations for sexual-harassment prevention at events and in online communities.
- The Consensus 2026 sponsor list and the "$90,000" figure come from secondary summaries and conflict with Consensys's statement. Check the originals (Fortune, Bloomberg, FT) before citing.
